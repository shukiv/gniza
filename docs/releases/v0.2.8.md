# v0.2.8

## A restore reserves what it writes

A 48.7 GiB account was asking for **176.5 GiB** of staging before it
would restore anything. On a server with 63.2 GiB free that meant nothing
could be restored from that account at all — not a rehearsal, not one
database out of it, not a single file.

Every restore was sized as the most expensive thing a restore can do,
whether or not it did it. Three full copies of the account plus a
gigabyte, plus the safety margin. Measuring found that no restore Gniza
performs costs three copies:

| What you asked for | Room needed now |
|---|---|
| **Restore one thing**, or picked files | about what those parts come to |
| Rebuild it for me to download | twice the account |
| Overwrite the live account | twice the account |
| The nightly rehearsal | **once** the account |

That 48.7 GiB account now rehearses in 59.6 GiB, and one 800 KiB database
comes out of it for the price of one 800 KiB database.

## An account is handed to cPanel as a directory

Applying a restore used to repack the rebuilt account into a `.tar` and
hand cPanel that. cPanel's first act was to extract it again — into a
temporary directory it makes right beside the file. So the volume held
the rebuilt tree, our tar of it, and cPanel's copy of the tar: three.

`restorepkg` takes a directory. Its own usage says so, and it copies one
into place with `cp --archive` exactly as it would extract an archive. It
is handed the directory now. One step shorter, one full copy of the
account cheaper, and nothing about what cPanel then does has changed.

## A rehearsal builds nothing to hand over

The nightly drill reads the account back, counts the files in the home
directory and parses every SQL dump. It never gives the result to
anybody. It was still packing a tar first, and that tar was the reason a
rehearsal cost twice the account.

It does not any more. A rehearsal now needs one copy of the account —
which is what makes it possible to *prove* a backup on a server too small
to restore it onto. Worth knowing on a full disk.

## What is not guessed

Sizing a small restore asks the backup what those paths come to. A
listing containing a **folder** is refused rather than added up: restic
lists a folder's direct children, so a 30 GiB `public_html` answers with
a few kilobytes of files at its top. Reserving a gigabyte for it would
pass the check and then fill the volume on a live server. Not knowing
falls back to the whole-account figure, which is always enough.

Databases, mailboxes, DNS zones, certificates, cron and FTP settings are
files. Those are sized exactly, and those are what *Restore one thing* is
for.

> Two claims on this page were not true when it shipped: a restore of
> **picked files** was still sized as a whole account, and a **mailbox**
> is a folder rather than a file, so it falls back like any other folder.
> [v0.2.9](v0.2.9.md) corrects both.
