# v0.3.5

## A lean DirectAdmin backup no longer carries the mailbox

A DirectAdmin backup that reads the home directory in place asks
DirectAdmin for everything but the account's own files, and every one of
those archives carried the whole mailbox anyway: 733 MB of messages in a
1.2 GB archive on one validation account, for an archive that should
have held its database dumps.

The task line named the options and nothing else. DirectAdmin took that
for a client written before `email_data` existed, and included the mail
as it always had. Its own backup form sends two more fields,
`email_data_aware=yes` and `database_data_aware=yes`, and with them the
same request came back at 15 MB.

Three things follow from it:

- The room reserved for such a backup is the databases, once. It was the
  mail and the databases, twice, which refused a 28.6 GiB account with
  10 GiB of databases on a disk with 16.5 GiB free.
- Roundcube's address books, identities and preferences are filed under
  `email_data`, so they leave with it. They are put back by hand:
  DirectAdmin's own `scripts/backup_roundcube.php` is run for every
  domain of the account, with an administrator's `scripts/custom/` copy
  ahead of it, into the member DirectAdmin's restore reads. A domain
  that cannot be exported is a warning on the backup, not a refusal.
- The per-mailbox send limits are filed there too, and are copied in
  from `/etc/virtual/<domain>/limit/`.

## A rebuilt archive restores its messages

The restore drill found that DirectAdmin's restore put back a rebuilt
archive's database, files and webmail contacts and left every message
behind, while its own archive of the same account put them back. The
difference is one file, `backup/<domain>/email/data/imap/.direct_imap_backup`,
which DirectAdmin's restore reads before it reads `imap/` and which its
own backup writes under `email_data`. The rebuild writes it now, for
every domain whose messages the archive carries. It is written when the
archive is rebuilt rather than when the backup is taken, so backups
already taken in this shape restore their mail as well.

## A restore is sized by the backup

Putting an account back was sized by the larger of the backup and the
live account, and the live account is what is on the disk now, part of
which the backup leaves out on purpose. A 7.1 GiB backup of a 24.4 GiB
account -- 12 GiB of it application backups -- asked for 49.9 GiB of
scratch on a disk with 21.9 GiB free. It is the backup's size now.

## One more directory left out by default

`wordpress-backups`, WP Toolkit's, joins `application_backups` and
`softaculous_backups`.
